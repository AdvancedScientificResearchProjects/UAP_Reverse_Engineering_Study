## Summary / Резюме

Section 10 is the **terminal volume of the FBI master case file 62-HQ-83894** — the closing-out chronological band that runs from late 1966 through May 1969, parallel to the formal closure of Project Blue Book (December 1969 Condon Report; Blue Book formally terminated 17 December 1969) and contemporaneous with AFSCA / Sr449 era. By 1966–69 the file is no longer logging incidents; it is **passively cataloging citizen-correspondence with NICAP, AFSCA, and various civilian-saucer organizations**, plus rare retroactive incident-related letters from idiosyncratic correspondents. The FBI's posture is now uniformly: the file matter is "not within the jurisdiction of the FBI"; standard practice is acknowledge receipt, route copy to USAF Office of Special Investigations, file.

The Hanawalt letter is a **textbook example of mid-1960s targeted-individual / mind-control delusional ideation** filed at FBI HQ-master-case level — possibly the most extensive single-correspondent paranoid-systematization document in the file.

The Stewart Moorestown NJ + Pan Am 707 Elkton MD sub-thread (transcript lines 8993-9245) is a 1969 retrospective filing of a 1963 incident. **Edward A. Stewart Jr.** (152 Mount Holly Avenue, Mount Holly NJ) wrote to Hoover claiming that on 7 December 1963 he observed an "unusual object in the sky at Moorestown, New Jersey ... and its unusual behavior. On December 8, 1963, just 24 hours later at Elkton, Maryland, the Pan American Aircraft with 73 passengers and a crew of eight perished when this aircraft exploded in mid air and crashed." Stewart claims the object was "laying air mines" that destroyed Pan Am 707-121 N709PA. He cites the CAB's 1965 official report (File 1-0015) which attributed the crash to a lightning strike, but quotes its statement that "Metal splatters on the leading edge of the horizontal stabilizer were identified by spectrographic analysis as being formed from two different aluminum alloys. However, these alloys could not be identified since the chemical composition of the deposits did not conform to any alloy used in the airplane." Stewart concludes the missiles are "Russia['s] ... constantly being sent over both the North and South Americas" disguised to look like meteors or flying saucers; "These objects are constantly being sent over both the North and South Americas by Russia. Anyone seeing these objects are instantly responsive as to a flying saucer. Subjected to ridicule if this be mentioned to any one ... Russia is well aware of this fact and has capitalized upon this fact. They have in this way been able to penetrate our Radar System undetected ... Now we are about to pay with 60,000,000 American lives or more. 'Some price'!" Stewart sends copies to President Nixon, Hoover, Senator Case (NJ), Senator Charles Percy (IL), and Melvin Laird (Secretary of Defense). Includes a 28 July 1964 Harvard College Observatory letter from Richard B. Southworth politely informing Stewart that an earlier observation (18 July 1964) "was a bright meteor, sometimes called a 'fireball' or 'bolide' ... fragments of asteroids" — Stewart insists his theory is correct anyway.
