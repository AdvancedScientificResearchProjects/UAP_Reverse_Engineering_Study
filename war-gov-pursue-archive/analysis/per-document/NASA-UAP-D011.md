## Summary / Резюме

An 8-minute 14-second audio excerpt from Mercury Atlas 9 (Faith 7) covering pilot L. Gordon Cooper Jr.'s real-time description of the "brilliant blue of sunrise beneath the haze layer of the Earth's atmosphere." Per AARO's description, as Cooper approaches sunrise he describes small, luminous, brilliant white particles drifting away from the spacecraft; he refers to "fireflies" specifically after deploying mission **beacons** — spherical mission-related equipment with xenon strobe lights. This frames at least part of the MA-9 "fireflies" observation as **operationally-attributable to a piece of deployed mission hardware** rather than to the spacecraft-condensation explanation alone, distinguishing the D011 segment from the D010 Glenn-reference moment.

**RU:** Аудиовыборка 8 минут 14 секунд из Mercury Atlas 9 (Faith 7), охватывающая описание Cooper в реальном времени "блестящего синего восхода под дымкой атмосферы Земли". По описанию AARO, по мере приближения Cooper к восходу он описывает небольшие, светящиеся, блестяще-белые частицы, дрейфующие от аппарата; он отсылает к "светлячкам" конкретно после развёртывания миссионных **маяков** — сферического миссионного оборудования с ксеноновыми стробоскопами. Это формулирует по крайней мере часть наблюдения "светлячков" MA-9 как **операционно-атрибутируемую развёрнутому миссионному оборудованию**, а не только спутниково-конденсатному объяснению, отличая сегмент D011 от момента отсылки к Glenn в D010.

D011 is the longer of the two MA-9 audio segments (494 s vs. D010's 180 s) and is structurally distinct: it captures both a **terrestrial-optical** observation (sunrise haze) and a **deployed-hardware "fireflies"** observation (the beacons), which is an operationally clean attribution. AARO's wording is unusually informative here — Cooper's "fireflies" *after deploying beacons* explicitly identifies the source as the xenon-strobe-equipped beacon hardware, which is a different attribution pathway from the general MA-6 / D010 condensation explanation. The two segments together (D010 + D011) provide a multi-source picture of "fireflies" during a single mission: (a) spacecraft condensation, (b) crew-perception inheritance from Glenn's terminology, and (c) deployed-beacon hardware. This is the most analytically rich Mercury-era audio pair in PURSUE.

**RU:** D011 — более длинный из двух аудиосегментов MA-9 (494 с против 180 с в D010) и структурно отличный: он захватывает как **земное-оптическое** наблюдение (дымка восхода), так и **развёрнуто-аппаратное "светлячковое"** наблюдение (маяки), что является операционно чистой атрибуцией. ## Notes / Замечания

- **Mission significance:** MA-9 / Faith 7 was the final Project Mercury mission; Cooper's flight included deliberate **flashing-beacon visibility experiments** (one of MA-9's documented mission objectives — testing whether a deployed visible beacon could be acquired against the Earth's night side). This is the operational context for the "after deploying beacons" framing — the beacons were planned, deployed mission equipment, not anomalous hardware.
- **NASA audio format / Mercury voice loop:** Mercury-program air-to-ground voice loops are public via NASA Archives; AARO's release surfaces this specific excerpt as UAP-relevant despite the self-resolving (deployed-hardware) attribution in the same description.
- **Crew biographical context — Cooper's post-NASA UFO profile:** Cooper is the most-publicly-vocal Mercury Seven astronaut on UFO topics — he gave on-record interviews late in life describing UFO observations from his Air Force service in Germany (1951) and post-flight regarding MA-9. The verbatim D010 + D011 audio is the **primary contemporaneous record** against which his retrospective claims can be checked.
- **R01 → R02 numerical continuation and the duplicate-segment pattern:** D010 + D011 represent the **first known case** in the NASA-UAP cluster of two AARO release codes assigned to a single mission's audio (segmented). This may indicate that AARO's release pipeline preferred fixed maximum durations (~500 s) per asset, forcing long mission audio into multiple coded segments. If so, the D008 (470 s, Apollo 12 medical) is at the same length ceiling — consistent with a soft cap rather than coincidence.
