## Summary / Резюме

A 26-second audio excerpt from the recovery phase of Mercury-Redstone 4 (Liberty Bell 7), in which the **recovery team discusses a dye pack in the water that did not activate.** Per AARO's description, the audio scope is the recovery operation following splashdown, not the suborbital flight itself. The dye-pack discussion is a routine recovery-operations communication — fluorescein dye markers were standard Mercury recovery equipment used to enhance visual detection of the capsule from helicopters; a non-activated dye pack is an equipment-performance observation, not a UAP-phenomenology one.

**RU:** Аудиовыборка 26 секунд из фазы восстановления Mercury-Redstone 4 (Liberty Bell 7), в которой **спасательная команда обсуждает пакет с красителем в воде, который не активировался**. По описанию AARO охват аудио — операция восстановления после приводнения, а не сам суборбитальный полёт. Обсуждение красителя — рутинная коммуникация по операциям восстановления — флуоресцеиновые маркеры красителя были стандартным оборудованием восстановления Mercury, используемым для усиления визуального обнаружения капсулы с вертолётов; неактивированный пакет красителя — наблюдение по производительности оборудования, а не феноменологии UAP.

D014 is the **earliest** NASA-UAP audio record in the entire PURSUE corpus and the **shortest** at 26 seconds. Phenomenologically it is the **least UAP-relevant** entry in the R02 NASA cluster: it contains no astronaut observation of unexplained phenomena, no "fireflies" lineage content, and no in-flight observation at all — only recovery-team radio chatter about a piece of marker hardware. Its inclusion in the AARO release is anomalous within the cluster and may reflect (a) AARO's curation criteria explicitly including any **historical** mission-recovery audio with **any** "unexplained-equipment-behavior" content, or (b) the dye-pack non-activation being read by AARO as procedurally adjacent to UAP investigation methodology, or (c) the MR-4 Liberty Bell 7 famous capsule-loss incident being itself the contextual hook for surfacing any associated audio. The shortness of the excerpt (26 s) further suggests an intentionally narrow editorial cut.

## Notes / Замечания

- **Mission significance:** MR-4 / Liberty Bell 7 was the second American crewed spaceflight (after Shepard's MR-3 / Freedom 7 on May 5, 1961) and the **last suborbital Mercury flight**. The mission is historically remembered almost entirely for the **post-splashdown loss of the capsule** when the explosive hatch fired prematurely; Grissom himself nearly drowned. The capsule was recovered from the Atlantic seafloor by an expedition in 1999. The dye-pack discussion in D014 falls within the post-splashdown recovery window in which this incident unfolded.
- **NASA audio format / Mercury recovery loop:** Mercury-program recovery communications (helicopter ↔ ship ↔ Mercury Control) are publicly available via NASA Archives; AARO surfaces this very short excerpt as UAP-relevant despite the routine equipment-discussion content.
- **Crew biographical context — Grissom:** Grissom is uniquely the **only Mercury Seven astronaut to die on duty** — he was killed in the Apollo 1 fire on January 27, 1967 (Cmdr of AS-204). MR-4 is his only completed spaceflight in the PURSUE corpus (his Gemini 3 flight, March 1965, is not in PURSUE). The historical weight of Grissom's mission record makes any associated audio in the AARO release historically significant, even when the content itself is procedural.
- **R01 → R02 numerical continuation:** D014 is **chronologically the earliest** NASA-UAP record in the entire PURSUE corpus (Jul 21, 1961), yet **numerically the last** of the R02 cluster (D008–D014). The mismatch underscores that the R02 D-series numbering is publication-order, not mission-chronology.
